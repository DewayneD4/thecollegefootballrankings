How the odds are calculated

  • Standings. Conference records count games ESPN marks as conference games between two members, played before the title game (so, for example, Army–Navy in December doesn’t count toward the American race). Membership and divisions come from ESPN’s FBS standings.
  • Game odds. Each remaining game uses a logistic curve on the gap in Defoor Ratings scores plus home field: P(home win) = 1 / (1 + e−(0.0371 × rating gap + 0.370 × home)). Home field is worth about 10.0 rating points, and a 10-point edge at a neutral site is about a 59% favorite. Coefficients: fit on 578 games this season (each week's ratings predicting the next week's results; 66% of winners called).
  • Simulation. The rest of the conference schedule is played 20,000 times (fixed random seed, so a rerun gives the same numbers). Teams are ranked by conference win percentage; the top two meet, or the two division winners in conferences with divisions. FBS Independents have no title game.
  • Tiebreakers (a close approximation of the conferences’ published procedures): (1) head-to-head among the tied teams, counted only if all of them played each other, otherwise a team that beat all the others advances and one that lost to all the others drops; (2) win percentage vs. common conference opponents; (3) record vs. common opponents, starting with the best-placed one; (4) combined conference win percentage of all conference opponents; (5) Defoor rating, standing in for the analytics or SportSource ranking the conferences use as a late step. Once one team is separated from a multi-team tie, the rest start over at step 1.
  • Title game. Played at a neutral site unless ESPN lists a campus site, in which case the higher seed is the home team. A matchup ESPN has already announced is locked in, and a completed title game shows the champion.
  • Clinched / eliminated marks come from a strict win-percentage check that ignores tiebreakers (or from ESPN’s announced matchup), so a team can show >99.9% before it officially clinches.
